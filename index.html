Hopper Demo

Hopper Demo is BDR's scale testbed for the systems a flight vehicle needs: landing suspension, guidance and control, thrust vectoring, flight dynamics, and flight computers. It lets the team prove those systems at small scale, where mistakes are cheap, before they're trusted on full-size hardware.

As team lead, most of my work happens at the interfaces. Big Dumb Rockets / Team Lead

Capacitive Fill

You can't look inside a tank of liquid oxygen, but during loading you need to know exactly how full it is. Capacitive Fill is our answer: a level sensor built into the tank. Liquid and vapor have different dielectric constants, so as the liquid rises past the sensor its capacitance changes, and that change tells you the fill level.

I led a team of three from design through manufacturing and integration, and worked with machinists to fit the sensor into model tanks. In testing it tracked the fill level from empty to full to within ±5%.

  • Cryogenics
  • Sensors

Big Dumb Rockets / Propulsion

Regen Mk II

Regen Mk II is the next step from Mk I: a regeneratively cooled Ethanol/LOX engine designed for long-duration, throttleable, gimbaled burns. In a regen engine the fuel runs through channels in the chamber wall before it's injected, so it carries away heat that would otherwise melt the engine. Longer burns, throttling, and gimbaling each make that thermal and structural balance harder.

I contributed structural analysis, helping determine that our current thrust plate was incapable of withstanding the loads that our new Regen Mk II engine would put on it. I drafted up a plan for how to better improve the design and passed my results off to the engineer reponsible for designing it.

  • FEA
  • CFD
  • Propulsion

Big Dumb Rockets / Propulsion

Regen Mk I

Before an engine fires, every part has to be checked against what it's supposed to be. On Mk I, I did hands-on integration and acceptance testing: inspecting components, verifying the assembly, cutting gaskets, machining the injector manifold, and LOX-cleaning the plumbing. That last step matters because any oil or debris left in an oxygen line can ignite.

On test day I was on site helping troubleshoot problems as they came up, and the engine completed a successful hot-fire at about 800 lbf of thrust. Afterward we took it apart for post-test teardown analysis, reading the hardware for what the firing did to it and carrying those lessons into Mk II.

  • LOX Handling
  • Acceptance Testing
  • Hot-Fire

Solo Project

Jul – Oct 2026

RC Jet

I spent a summer taking an aircraft through the whole design cycle by myself: a ducted-fan RC jet that I designed, simulated, built, and flew. The airframe was modeled in Onshape, the wing was sized against FEA wing-loading results from SOLIDWORKS, and flow analysis in flow5 put the top speed at about 50 mph. The first flight went well, achieving a peak altitude of about 65 feet, though I had no altimeter on board so I used the building I was flying by as my reference.

The whole airframe is 3D printed, which made it fast to iterate. It took three design revisions to get to a flight-ready version. On the first powered flight it stayed up for 30 seconds and landed safely in the grass.

  • Onshape
  • Solidworks Simulation
  • flow5
  • 3D Printing
